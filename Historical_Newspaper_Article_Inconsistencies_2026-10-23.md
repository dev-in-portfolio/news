# Historical Newspaper Archives — cross-article inconsistency audit

**Checkpoint:** 23 October 2026 (America/New_York)  
**Corpus:** 1,056 fixed HN source records; original master workbook left untouched.  
**Stage:** **Preliminary cross-source claim audit of existing research notes. Not an exhaustive comparison of every original article.**

## What was actually checked

- Reconciled **44 saved batch reports** (43 distinct ranges; HN-403–427 was researched twice) against the 1,056-row master seed queue. Every HN identifier belongs to a saved batch interval; this establishes *documented research coverage*, not that all articles for it were found or fully transcribed.
- Manually distinguished **84 story-level inconsistencies, revisions, disputed claims, or source/data holds** in the saved notes: **17 M; 19 R; 11 P; 14 Q; 8 F; 15 V**. The classes overlap in reality but this register assigns one principal class per HN source row.
- Separately flagged **105 additional HN rows** by text-pattern screening. These are *search prompts*, not proven disagreements. The original workbook's **140 candidate identity pairs** also remain *unresolved links*, not automatic event merges.
- **Reopened both Black Tom July 31, 1916 OCR newspaper pages**, both September 20, 1985 Mexico earthquake pages, and the January 4, 2006 Sago mine reporting in the Los Angeles Times and Washington Post. These claim-level spot checks confirm their stated numeric or naming differences. Other statements generally rely on the earlier saved A/B evidence and need rechecking for final certification.
- A = earlier report says original article body/dated issue was accessible; B = title/date/headline/index only, article body unread; C/retro = context or later retrospective, **not a second contemporary newspaper**. Early 001–277 use P/I/N nomenclature, broadly inspected original / identified issue / no additional source, and should not be flattened into later A/B without review.

### Legend for inconsistency classes

| Code | What it means | Can we call the newspapers demonstrably inconsistent? |
|---|---|---|
| **V** | Two or more printed counts/wordings differ, or contemporaneous coverage prints incompatible figures | Only where original-text evidence is A; B-only contrasts remain pending. A difference may be rationally explained by time, definition or attribution. |
| **R** | Earlier assertion was corrected, retracted or revised | Yes as a **time-stamped record of changing information**, not a permanent factual contradiction. |
| **P** | Competing accusations, eyewitness accounts, causes or editorial positions | Their claims differ; **neither is automatically true**. |
| **M** | Date, place, identity, catalog, seed heading, or procedural description does not align | May be *dataset/metadata* inconsistency rather than two newspapers. |
| **F** | Seemingly incompatible values use different dates, units, denominators or stages | **Not proven to be a contradiction**. Kept to prevent misleading syntheses. |
| **Q** | Notes warn that source accounts differ, but a line-by-line numeric/claim extract is missing | **Open verification**, not a confirmed divergence. |

## Most concrete cross-publication discrepancies

1. **Black Tom, HN-403 (31 July 1916):** *Morning Oregonian* reports **three known dead, at least five missing**; *Eugene Daily Guard* reports **four dead, eleven to twenty missing**. This audit checked both original OCR pages. The former gives **$25 million** loss, the latter an estimated **$25–45 million** range. These are different dispatch snapshots, not verified final losses. [Morning Oregonian](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn83025138/1916-07-31/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/) / [Eugene Daily Guard](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088107/1916-07-31/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/).
2. **Chicago violence, HN-416 (1 August 1919):** original Indiana seed **32 deaths**, *Eugene Daily Guard* **34 deaths** on the same publication date. Still need press time/wire dateline for the count's progression.
3. **Tulsa, HN-426 (1–3 June 1921):** identified original issue headlines range **56, 75, 80 and around 100 deaths**. The **80** figure appears on an accessible *County Record* original; 56, 75, 100 are Library of Congress-curated **issue leads B** and still need original page text read. Blame attribution in hostile local and Black press requires careful, source-specific comparisons.
4. **Hartford circus fire, HN-545 (8 July 1944):** seed **153** versus same-day *Eugene Register-Guard* **152**; next-day *Oregon Statesman* says **over 150**, which is consistent with either.
5. **Bikini Able, HN-557 (1 July 1946):** seed says **20 ships damaged**; *Midland Reporter-Telegram* says **3 sunk and 8 damaged**; *Wanganui Herald* initially described ships looking little changed. The measures and observation times differ; the Baker underwater test occurred later and is excluded.
6. **Texas City, HN-562 (17 April 1947):** the same *Medford Mail Tribune* page quotes **714** (Red Cross), **600–650** (governor), and **~400 bodies recovered**; *Pampa News* estimates **650**. Reporting source and denominator must accompany every number.
7. **Silver Bridge, HN-666 (16–18 December 1967):** *Point Pleasant Register* headline **16 dead, 44 missing** versus *Charleston Gazette-Mail* **8 dead, 46 missing**. **Both issue-level headlines B, not articles opened**, so a documented metadata headline contrast, *not yet* certified article-text comparison.
8. **Mexico City quake, HN-753 (20 September 1985):** *Lawton Morning Press* attributes **at least 170** to police/AP; Indiana seed says approximately **230**; *Muskogee Daily Phoenix* attributes **at least 250** to Mexico City's Channel 13 **and separately prints AP's at-least-170 figure in the same article**. I checked both original Oklahoma OCR pages. Thus the two figures are *contemporaneous competing estimates explicitly reported within one newspaper*, not merely different papers or logically incompatible minimums.
9. **USS Stark, HN-766 (19–20 May 1987):** *Los Angeles Times* and *Muskogee Daily Phoenix* **28 dead** versus later *Washington Post* **37 dead or presumed dead**—an explicitly documented upward revision, with 'presumed' status preserved.
10. **Sago mine, HN-894 (January 2006):** falsely conveyed **12 survivors** reversed to **12 dead and one survivor**; *Los Angeles Times* calls the survivor **Ronald McCloy**, while the *Washington Post* identifies **Randal McCloy**. These are two separate corrections, the second a given-name error.
11. **Deepwater Horizon, HN-920 (May 2010):** contemporary *Washington Post* directly contrasts **5,000 and 26,000 barrels/day**, and *Los Angeles Times* describes an independent estimate above the government's 5,000. Methodological dispute, not established final outflow.
12. **Pulse, HN-952 (June 2016):** **50 fatalities counting the shooter** versus **49 murdered victims excluding him**. The denominator changes; it does **not** imply a victim count was secretly revised downward.
13. **Camp Fire, HN-965 (9 November 2018):** same-day *San Francisco Chronicle* **at least 5 dead** versus *Los Angeles Times* **at least 9**. Successive provisional reports.
14. **Rhodes flood, HN-1053 (October 2026):** Greek account gives reported victim age **25**, while a later inaccessible/index-only paper gives **62**. **Unresolved B/hold**; do not assign either as fact. Same seed article ALSO combines this Greece disaster with an *unrelated Mexican hurricane*, an event-boundary inconsistency.

### Article-text checks completed in this audit

- **HN-403:** The [31 July Morning Oregonian](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn83025138/1916-07-31/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/) says three known dead and five or more missing; the [31 July Eugene Daily Guard](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088107/1916-07-31/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/) says four dead and eleven to twenty missing. The Oregonian's dispatch is dated New York July 30, which helps explain the next day's change.
- **HN-753:** The [Lawton Morning Press original page](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc2568378/m1/1/) says police estimated at least 170 dead. The [Muskogee Daily Phoenix original page](https://gateway.okhistory.org/ark:/67531/metadc2641966/m1/1/) attributes at least 250 to Channel 13, while ALSO giving the AP tally of at least 170. This makes the source-attribution difference explicit.
- **HN-894:** The [Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2006-jan-04-na-miners4-story.html) spells the lone survivor Ronald McCloy. The [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/business/technology/2006/01/04/12-found-dead-in-wva-coal-mine/0c7d2ad1-36e3-4ee9-9f33-b730fcac355f/) spells his name Randal McCloy and documents the rescue team's report being mistakenly interpreted as 12 survivors. The name difference and correction sequence are directly visible in these original articles.
