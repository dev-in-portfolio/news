About the Archive / Methodology

What this collection is, how sources are gathered, the A/B/C evidence language, limitations, corrections, and contact information.

What this archive is

A living, interactive historical newspaper research and discovery archive covering 1777–2026. The central experience is seeing what newspapers actually printed and how reports evolved across time and publications.

The initial collection contains 1,056 HN research/source records, not necessarily 1,056 distinct historical events. Multiple HN rows may be separate reporting stages or overlapping records of one event.

Editorial commitments

  • Newspaper report means a historical publication made a claim; it does not establish that claim as fact.
  • Separate contemporary reporting, later scholarly/historical context, and our own analytical assessment.
  • Preserve exact attribution, publication dates, dispatch dates, time zones, numerical units, uncertainty, and later revisions.
  • Distinguish genuine incompatibility from evolving estimates, changed denominators, attribution disputes, metadata mistakes, and multiple stages of the same event.
  • Do not frame the collection as evidence of a cover-up or suppression. Avoid sensationalist certainty.
  • Original scanned articles are the primary visual artifact wherever legitimately available.

Evidence language (A / B / C)

  • A: Accessible/inspected original article or primary page, as justified by the underlying source report.
  • B: Identified dated issue/headline/index or catalog lead, but article content uninspected.
  • C: Unresolved candidate, secondary/retrospective context, or ambiguous association.
  • Early batches use P/I/N naming; those codes are preserved and mapped to A/B/C only after explicit review.

Inconsistency classes (V / R / P / M / F / Q)

  • V: Printed counts/wordings differ. Requires same-measure attribution and date context before calling it an incompatibility.
  • R: Reporting was corrected/retracted/revised. Show the chronology.
  • P: Competing accusations, explanations, eyewitness accounts or editorials. Neither side is presumed true.
  • M: Metadata/identity/date/place/catalog mismatch; may be source-data error rather than a newspaper contradiction.
  • F: Apparently conflicting values measure different things, moments, populations, or denominators; do not label as false reporting.
  • Q: Potential divergence mentioned in notes but lacking original-text comparison; remains open.

Curated flags (84) and heuristic keyword leads (105) are tracked separately and never summed into a "confirmed contradiction" count.

Source-first media policy

Authentic article scans/crops are displayed only where acquisition and permitted reuse allow. Where permissions or access are unclear, an external source link is used instead of an unauthorized copied image. No AI-generated images, fabricated archive photos, or altered newspaper typography are used as historical evidence.

Corrections

Story pages show the most recent reviewed date and distinguish newly added evidence from corrections to prior interpretations. A visible correction/contact route is provided. Historical record and earlier published interpretations are preserved for audit without silently changing source evidence. Private research notes are never exposed in public builds.

No live deployment has been authorized. A later possible path is darkstarconsultinggroup.com/newspaper-archives, but it is not an established deployment target.
