Sago mine disaster

Sago, West Virginia, January 2-4, 2006 — a false "12 survivors" report corrected to 12 dead and one survivor, with a survivor-name discrepancy between two newspapers.

Evidence tier A — original article opened and read Audit class R — reporting corrected/retracted/revised LA Times source identity verified Washington Post inaccessible (fetch timeout)

HN-894 · Event dates: January 2-4, 2006 · Location: Sago Mine, near Tallmansville, Upshur County, WV · Classification: Disasters, Accidents & Emergencies · Two separate corrections: the survivor-count reversal, and a given-name error.

What happened (attributed, with correction chronology)

At a glance: On Monday, January 2, 2006, an explosion trapped 13 miners at the Sago Mine. Rescue efforts ran for roughly 41 hours. Early reports conveyed that 12 miners had survived — this was reversed when officials announced that 12 were dead and only one had survived. The two newspapers of record also spelled the lone survivor's first name differently.

  • False "12 survivors" report: Word that rescuers had found 12 men alive spread rapidly; families celebrated at Sago Baptist Church. About three hours later, officials announced only one miner — Ronald/Randal McCloy — had been transported alive to the hospital.
  • International Coal Group (ICG) president Ben Hatfield attributed the error to a lack of communication: rescuers had reported finding multiple survivors and checking vital signs, and the information "spread like wildfire" from the command center.
  • 13th miner: The body of a 13th miner had been found earlier, leading to the grim speculation that the others were probably also dead.

Comparison — correction chronology and name discrepancy

Audit class R (earlier assertion corrected, retracted, or revised). This is a time-stamped record of changing information, not a permanent factual contradiction:

  • Count reversal: The false report of "12 survivors" was reversed to "12 dead and one survivor" — a documented upward revision in fatalities and downward revision in survivors as the rescue command center's information was corrected.
  • Name discrepancy: Los Angeles Times spells the lone survivor Ronald McCloy; the Washington Post (per the audit register) identifies him as Randal McCloy. This is a second, separate correction — a given-name error. Neither spelling is presumed correct without checking the original page.

Preserve the correction chronology and the source-level name discrepancy. Do not present either newspaper's spelling as established fact without checking the original page image.

Reporting timeline (what they knew when)

January 2, 2006 (morning) — Explosion traps 13 miners at Sago Mine; rescue efforts begin.
January 2-3, 2006 (night) — Word spreads that 12 miners survived; families celebrate at Sazi Baptist Church; body of a 13th miner recovered earlier.
January 3, 2006 (early morning) — Officials reverse: only one miner survived; 12 dead. ICG's Ben Hatfield attributes the error to a lack of communication from the rescue command center.
January 4, 2006 — Los Angeles Times and Washington Post report the correction and the survivor-name discrepancy (Ronald vs Randal McCloy).
Later reporting — Investigations into the mine's safety-record citations and the explosion's cause continued after the initial reporting.
