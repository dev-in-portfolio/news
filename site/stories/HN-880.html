Recorded finding

2004 Beslan reporting contested labeling assailants 'militants' vs 'terrorists' and use of images/headlines.

Discovery narrative

Source: Jewish Post, p. 35 — original media-criticism column; European Court of Human Rights case-law summary.
Report: A columnist condemned perceived journalistic euphemisms for the gunmen in the Beslan, Russia school siege, stating that some 400 people had died and comparing words used by international newsrooms.
Assessment: A polemic about terminology and an approximate early casualty figure, not a final victim register or impartial media study. The European Court's later case summary describes approximately 334 civilian deaths, including 186 children; detailed responsibility and the state's response remained subjects of legal review.
