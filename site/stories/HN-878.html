Recorded finding

FBI's Madrid 2004 fingerprint match tied Brandon Mayfield to attack; Spain questioned it and FBI later withdrew identification.

Discovery narrative

Source: Jewish Post, p. 11 — original commentary; Justice Department inspector-general investigation.
Report: The commentary described American lawyer Brandon Mayfield being arrested after FBI fingerprint examiners said his print matched evidence tied to the March 11 Madrid train bombings, then released when Spanish officials attributed the print to another man. The writer also debated whether religious profiling shaped the case.
Assessment: The erroneous fingerprint identification is firmly documented by the DOJ inspector general. Claims about the precise relative influence of religion, examiner methodology, and investigative judgment must be tied to the actual review, not decided merely by the commentary's politics. Mayfield was not a proven Madrid bomber.
