Source: Indianapolis Times, p. 1 — original page; FBI Lindbergh-kidnapping history.
Report: The day after his execution, a United Press account said Bruno Richard Hauptmann had gone to the electric chair without confessing, again insisting he was innocent of the Lindbergh baby's kidnapping and murder. The dispatch portrayed the case as an enduring controversy.
Assessment: The 1935 conviction (HN-492) and April 3, 1936 execution are documented events. The condemned man's declaration and the newspaper's characterization are not a newly established judicial finding of innocence or guilt; competing claims about the prosecution require the evidence record, not the last-minute headline alone.
