Recorded finding

Black Tom 31 July 1916: Morning Oregonian THREE known dead and at least FIVE missing versus Eugene Daily Guard FOUR killed and ELEVEN–TWENTY missing.

Discovery narrative

Source: Evening Republican, p. 2 — original page; National Park Service history.
Report: Explosions destroyed ammunition stores on Black Tom Island near Jersey City, smashed windows for miles, and caused fires. The first dispatch guessed $25 million in damage, reported three confirmed deaths and at least five missing, and acknowledged that casualty lists could not yet be checked.
Assessment: The National Park Service identifies the event as deliberate wartime sabotage of munitions bound for the Allies. The initial loss estimates, missing-person counts, and speculation in the paper should not be mistaken for later verified totals or an identification of individual saboteurs in this first report.
