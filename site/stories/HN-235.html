Discovery narrative

Source: Rensselaer Union and Jasper Republican, p. 2 — original page.
Report: An extraordinary reprinted account concerned William Hutchison, a farmer near Springfield in Erie County, Pennsylvania, who purportedly experienced violent, largely painless convulsions during a two-week period every year, beginning about 1858. The reporter claimed sedatives and changes of climate failed to stop the attacks and that numerous doctors had witnessed them.
Assessment: An unusual nineteenth-century medical claim, not a verified diagnosis or paranormal phenomenon. The writer repeatedly asserted its truth, but no original clinical case notes, page images establishing every figure, or independent follow-up have been checked. Follow-up open.
