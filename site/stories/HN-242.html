Discovery narrative

Source: Democratic Sentinel, p. 2 — original page.
Report: Reporting research prompted by the deadly Minneapolis flour-mill explosion, the paper described Professors Peck and Peckham of the University of Minnesota blowing finely divided flour and mill dust through flame, comparing bran, middlings, and other grinding byproducts. It distinguished dust suspended in air from compact material and noted that a glowing wire and some sparks failed to ignite dust under their test conditions, while direct flame could.
Assessment: Contemporary experimental attempt to explain a major industrial disaster, not final proof of the exact ignition source in Minneapolis. This account cautions against reducing a complex dust explosion to a single untested spark theory. Technical follow-up open.
