Mexico City earthquake

Mexico City, September 20, 1985 — contemporaneous newspaper reporting with competing casualty estimates, including competing figures reported within a single newspaper article.

What the newspapers reported (attributed)

At a glance: On September 20, 1985, a major earthquake struck Mexico City. Contemporary U.S. newspapers printed differing casualty estimates. These are contemporary reports — "at least" figures are lower bounds, not logically incompatible totals.

  • Lawton Morning Press (Oklahoma) — reported police estimated at least 170 dead, attributed to police/AP.
  • Muskogee Daily Phoenix (Oklahoma) — attributed at least 250 dead to Mexico City's Channel 13, while also printing AP's tally of at least 170 dead in the same article.
  • Indiana seed record: approximately 230 dead.

The two figures are contemporaneous competing estimates explicitly reported within one newspaper (Muskogee Daily Phoenix prints both Channel 13's 250 and AP's 170), not merely different papers or logically incompatible minimums. Both original Oklahoma OCR pages were checked in the audit pass.

Comparison notes

Audit class V (printed counts/wordings differ). The 170 vs 250 figures are "at least" lower bounds reported by different partial/dated sources; they do not logically contradict one another. The audit's key insight: the Muskogee Daily Phoenix article itself carried both estimates (Channel 13's 250 and AP's 170), so this is a case of competing contemporaneous estimates reported side-by-side — not a clean newspaper-vs-newspaper contradiction. Same-measure attribution and date context are required before calling this an incompatibility.

Reporting timeline (what they knew when)

September 20, 1985 — Major earthquake strikes Mexico City; casualty estimates begin to circulate via AP and wire services.
September 20-21, 1985 — Lawton Morning Press prints police/AP estimate of at least 170 dead.
September 20-21, 1985 — Muskogee Daily Phoenix prints Channel 13 estimate of at least 250 dead, and separately prints AP's at-least-170 figure in the same article.
Later reporting — Final confirmed death tolls were established weeks/months later as recovery progressed. Contemporary "at least" figures are lower bounds and are not revised downward as error.
