# Historical newspaper research — article-finding pass HN-378–402

**Date:** October 23, 2026  
**Scope:** 25 consecutive *HN-numbered source rows* (HN-378 through HN-402) in the established 1,056-row newspaper discovery tracker. The tracker has **two Titanic rows, HN-382 and HN-383, both bearing provisional packet ID EV-HN-382**. Both are retained individually below. There is no unique EV-HN-383 packet in those two rows; the original workbook is unchanged.

**Research objective:** Find individual, contemporary **newspaper appearances across publications and issue dates** associated with each discovery. This remains a *finding pass*, not a narrative retelling, a claim of exhaustive publication coverage, or confirmation of casualty estimates, accusations, or causes in early reports.

## Reading the results

- **A — inspected contemporary appearance:** a dated original newspaper page, archive scan/PDF, OCR text, or article transcription found to discuss the relevant event or its continuing contemporary reporting. Every A record identifies a newspaper and date and links the article or specific original page.
- **B — identified publication lead:** a specific newspaper/date relevant to the event, located through a contemporary-issue catalog or historical index, without independent inspection of the particular full article. B items **are not recovered/read newspaper articles**.
- The **original Indiana seed is not counted**. A newspaper issue is counted **once per HN row**, even if multiple related items appear in that issue. Separate issue dates count separately. Because HN-382/HN-383 are two **distinct seed angles on the same Titanic incident**, an article can relate to both conceptually; the lists below use different dated issue appearances without double-counting the same source URL across those two rows.
- Newspaper editors' casualty totals, initial theories, alleged perpetrators, and sensational descriptions are **claims preserved for historical press analysis**. They are not endorsed as accurate findings. Finding distinct reprints of a syndicated dispatch establishes distribution, not independent confirmation.
