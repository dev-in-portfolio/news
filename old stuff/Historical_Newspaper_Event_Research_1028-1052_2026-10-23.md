## Method and count discipline

The `Seed queue` in **Historical_Newspaper_Event_Research_Tracker_v0.1.xlsx** supplies the 25 event identifiers, dates and original seed URLs. The original Guardian story (or the two explicitly syndicated seed entries) **is not counted again**. This report finds **additional, distinct newspaper reporting**, not simply copies of a story on different hosts.

- **A** — a qualifying original newspaper/newsroom piece whose publisher article body was opened and meaningfully read; distinct original editorial pieces, not merely duplicated wire copy.
- **B** — the original newspaper article was specifically identified by headline, outlet and URL, but its full body was not available to inspect; **not certified as read**.
- **Excluded / held** — AP, Reuters, AFP, Bloomberg, dpa/APA syndication, newspaper republishing of those wires, the Guardian seed, broadcaster-only reports, science or trade magazines, institutional releases, speculative publisher relationships, and different events sharing a subject. Some potential local articles remain **unqualified candidates** if originality could not be established.
- Dates are the **tracker's event date**. A publisher article dated the following day may be valid if it covers the same occurrence. Article dates, death counts, preliminary measurements, claims and later revisions remain distinct.

**Batch result: 23 A + 8 B = 31 additional newspaper source leads across 25 seeds.** There are **three event packets with no additional qualifying newspaper source** in this pass: HN-1029, HN-1047 and HN-1050. That is a search/evidence gap, not evidence that the event did not occur or was not covered anywhere.

## Source evidence and packet notes

### HN-1028 — Ancient wrecks near Gibraltar (15 April)

- **A1 — SUR in English, 20 April:** [Spanish archaeologists discover ancient wreck inventory in the Bay of Gibraltar](https://www.surinenglish.com/andalucia/hidden-treasures-spanish-archaeologists-discover-trove-ancient-20260420145453-nt.html). Dilip Kuner newspaper article; article body read. Surveys identified underwater archaeological material and many wrecks from different eras.
- **Packet note:** The reported underwater sites and wreck counts are **survey inventory**, not one shipwreck or one newly excavated treasure trove. The article's similar phrasing to the seed is not counted as two different stories by the same outlet; it is one separately bylined local newspaper article.

### HN-1029 — Roman grave marker returned to Italy (1 May)

## Exclusion and quality-control trail

1. **Guardian seed articles:** 23 Guardian originals excluded from increment, plus the HN-1045 WCAX/AP and HN-1047 NY1/AP seed articles.
2. **Wire attribution:** Washington Post AP copy about US heat and the lunar crater; Hong Kong Standard AFP crater dispatch; German dpa and Austrian APA Enceladus articles; ANSA Italian wreck coverage; AP solar eclipse reposts and other regional syndications. A publisher masthead does not transform wire copy into independent reporting.
3. **Different event phase:** Roman marker's 2025 backyard discovery versus April 2026 repatriation; lunar crater's spring 2024 formation versus September 2026 identification; Roman telescope's launch versus first survey output; July 31 Naples main quake versus next-day swarm.
4. **Different study:** Enceladus simulated microbe survival versus another September plume-detection study; black-hole-star theory versus the unrelated September “burp” accretion-rate paper.
5. **Unverified independent authorship:** Boca Raton Tribune earthquake article and Il Quotidiano del Sud swarm article remain research leads outside A/B totals pending a direct publisher-body/byline check.
6. **Material factual conflict:** The late Times of India Roman marker retelling substituted a different person's name for **Sextus Congenius Verus**; excluded instead of silently treating it as confirmation.
