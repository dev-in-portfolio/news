# Historical Newspaper Event Research — HN-478 through HN-502

**Finding pass:** October 23, 2026  
**Range:** July 1932–August 1936; 25 established event IDs  
**Objective:** Find further contemporary newspaper publications about the 25 historical events already in the master list. This is a finding/retrieval ledger, **not** a narrative interpretation, a full transcription, or an exhaustive census of surviving articles.

## Evidence standard

- **A — located newspaper appearance:** A digitized original newspaper page/issue or facsimile is linked and contemporaneous article/headline/OCR content establishes coverage of the event. An A means the event-bearing page was *located*, **not** that we transcribed every line or assessed all the story's claims.
- **B — specific publication lead:** A named issue/article was surfaced by an archive catalog, historical citation, publisher retrospective, or historical-newspaper inventory, but the original event-bearing source was not independently inspected. A B is not a retrieved article.
- **Context:** Adjacent-stage material, helpful for a later broad search, but not confirmed as coverage of the precise event phase. It is kept outside A and B totals.
- **Counting:** One publication title per event, even if several pages of a single edition are linked. The 25 pre-existing Indiana master-seed newspaper items are **excluded**, and the same physical clipping presented on multiple websites is not counted twice. A shared newswire printed by two different papers is two **publication** appearances, not two independent witnesses.
- **Caution:** Death counts, criminal accusations, responsibility claims, and time-sensitive legal stages are what the contemporary publication reported at that point. This ledger does not convert preliminary figures or theories into final findings.

## Findings by event

### HN-478 — July 29, 1932 — Bonus Army clashes with Washington police

- **A1 — [*La Grande Evening Observer* (Oregon), July 29, 1932, p. 1](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/2006260039/1932-07-29/ed-1/seq-1.pdf).** Original front page reports the clash with officers, a veteran's death, federal instructions, and arrests.
- **Distinction:** The *Indianapolis Times* front-page photo is the master seed; it is not recounted. Arrests, shooting and subsequent encampment clearance are separate actions.

### HN-479 — July 30, 1932 — Bonus Army eviction/aftermath

- **A1 — [*Capital Journal* (Salem, Oregon), July 30, 1932, p. 1](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn90066132/1932-07-30/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/).** Contemporary follow-up reports troops returning to Fort Myer after the removal of veterans and quotes officials describing order restored.
- **A2 — [*Waterbury Democrat* (Connecticut), July 30, 1932, p. 1](https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn82014085/1932-07-30/ed-1/?sp=1&st=text).** Library of Congress newspaper issue discusses the Bonus Army and its dispersal.
- **Distinction:** An official description of a city returning to normal does not establish that the earlier clearance was peaceful.

### HN-480 — March 6, 1933 — Roosevelt's nationwide banking holiday

- **A1 — [*Bend Bulletin* (Oregon), March 6, 1933, p. 1](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn90051770/1933-03-06/ed-1/seq-1.pdf).** Original front page prints the national four-day bank-holiday proclamation news and associated scrip/credit arrangements.
- **A2 — [*Daily Worker* (New York), March 6, 1933, original issue PDF](https://www.marxists.org/history/usa/pubs/dailyworker/1933/v010-n055-NY-mar-06-1933-DW-LOC.pdf).** Period newspaper reporting bank-holiday conditions from a contrasting political viewpoint.
- **B1 — [*Coldwater Daily Reporter* (Michigan), March 6](https://www.rarenewspapers.com/newspaper/573095-1933-bank-holiday-ordered).** Dealer-recorded front-page nationwide closure headline.
- **B2 — [*Norwich Sun* (New York), March 6](https://www.rarenewspapers.com/newspaper/568989-1933-bank-holiday-ordered).** Dealer-recorded Roosevelt proclamation headline.
- **B3 — [*New York Times*, March 6](https://www.rarenewspapers.com/newspaper/687843-1933-bank-holiday-ordered).** Dealer inventory identifies the four-day closure headline and proclamation text; source page not independently inspected.
- **B4 — [*Omaha Bee-News* (Nebraska), March 6](https://www.rarenewspapers.com/newspaper/568988-1933-bank-holiday-ordered).** Period scrip-planning headline documented by original-newspaper inventory.
- **B5 — [*Fitchburg Sentinel* (Massachusetts), March 6](https://www.abebooks.com/magazines-periodicals/Bank-holiday-ordered/32516529323/bd).** Dated newspaper listing; full page needs inspection.
- **B6 — [*Cleveland News* (Ohio), March 6](https://www.rarenewspapers.com/newspaper/568906-1933-bank-holiday-ordered?imagelist=1).** Front-page inventory with local banking/payroll measures.
- **B7 — [*Daily Record* (Morris County, New Jersey), March 6](https://www.mclib.info/Research/Morris-County-Library-Reference-Guides/The-Great-Depression/1933-The-Great-Depression-January-March-1933).** County library's historic newspaper guide identifies the specific issue and its warning editorial.
- **Distinction:** The bank holiday was March 6; the Emergency Banking Act was March 9. Various state closures and reopening dates differ.

### HN-481 — March 27, 1933 — Allegations about responsibility for Reichstag fire

- **A0 / B0 verified additional event-specific newspaper appearances.** The original *Daily Banner* March 27 story is the master seed.
- **Context only:** [*Cleveland Press*, March 27, 1933, reproduced in a PBS historical press exhibit](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/fight-nazis-news-1933/) — contemporaneous treatment of Nazi actions toward Jews, not verified as repeating the precise Reichstag-arson allegation. The [U.S. State Department's Berlin report of March 27](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1933v02/d155) records a propaganda-ministry account, but it is a diplomatic record, **not a newspaper** and not counted.
- **Caution:** Neither Nazi statements nor opponents' counter-allegations establish the perpetrator without separate evidence.

### HN-482 — June 27, 1933 — Indiana ratifies repeal amendment

- **B1 — *Evansville Journal* (Indiana), June 27, 1933, p. 4.** AP report “Indiana Formally Votes For Prohibition Repeal,” specifically identified in [historical newspaper citations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-first_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution); source page not inspected. This is a *different paper* from the original *Decatur Daily Democrat* seed.
- **Distinction:** Indiana's convention acted June 26 and the dated press appeared June 27. This ratification by one state did not complete national repeal.

### HN-483 — September 21, 1933 — Reichstag fire trial begins

- **A1 — [*Straits Budget* (Singapore), September 28, 1933](https://eresources.nlb.gov.sg/newspapers/digitised/issue/straitsbudget19330928-1).** Original paper provides continuing coverage of the Reichstag fire defendants' trial, one week after opening.
- **Context only:** [Bulgarian Telegraph Agency's published archive](https://www.bta.bg/en/news/archives/1033608-december-23-1933-bulgarian-defendants-in-reichstag-fire-trial-acquitted) reproduces contemporary September 21 press-agency dispatches. These are useful original-period reports, but not a separately identified newspaper title and **not counted**.
- **Caution:** Proceedings begun September 21 are not verdicts. One-week-later reporting is dated as such, and the contemporary allegation of a Communist plot remains attributed.

### HN-484 — November 8, 1933 — Utah election prematurely described as end of Prohibition
