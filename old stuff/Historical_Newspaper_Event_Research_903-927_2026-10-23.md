## Scope and grading

- **A:** The substantive body of a period *newspaper* report, editorial, or opinion column was opened and read; its relevance is explained. This is a lead to independently reported newspaper coverage, not a ruling that every statement in the seed was correct.
- **B:** An independently relevant dated period newspaper item was identified but its substantive body was not accessible. A search excerpt alone never earns A.
- Count **one distinct independent newspaper title per seed**. Never count the original Indiana seed. Do not count stand-alone news agencies, modern retrospectives, broadcasts, magazines, government records, advocacy releases, or plain presidential speech transcripts. Republishing wire copy in a newspaper does not turn it into independent reporting.
- **Context-only** means that the source documents the general disaster, issue, or national trend *but not* the particular school, resident, student editorial, fundraiser, or local policy claimed by a seed. A context-only A is a lead for the larger story; it is not claim-specific corroboration.
- Historical newspaper dates, reported figures, alleged misconduct, pending litigation and proposed policies are handled as contemporary snapshots, not final results. Especially important: the May 2009 H1N1 casualty total changed while stories were being reported.
- The Indiana issue landing pages can load without exposing their underlying article text in web retrieval. Their *date, paper and seed heading* below come from the spreadsheet queue; opening a landing page is not claimed as having read the original article.
