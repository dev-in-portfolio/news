# Historical newspaper taxonomy — final one-story category review

**22 October 2026.** This is the final review of the 21 proposed third-level labels that originally had only one HN report. It supplements, rather than replaces, the 51-proposal decision ledger and the 1,056-record three-level classification.

## Decision

- **20 of the 21** original single-report proposals genuinely need a distinct third-level subject label and remain in the lock candidate.
- **1 of the 21** is redundant and is merged with a pre-existing subject: wartime censorship → **10.05 Press freedom** (HN-542).
- **0** of these single-report subjects should be deleted merely for having one example. Each retained label covers a coherent subject with meaningful potential for future articles; the distinctness test below was applied individually.
- The later extra singleton, **03.03 Policy debates and public response (HN-577)**, also merits retention; a policy-reaction interview is neither a laboratory discovery nor necessarily an enacted government action.
- **No changes to the prior candidate tree or 1,056-row classification are required as a result of this review.**

## Review method

A candidate earns its own third-level label only if (1) its single source actually discusses that subject, (2) the subject cannot accurately fit an existing sibling without stretching that sibling's meaning, (3) the label is reusable for future articles and is not just a unique event name, and (4) it doesn't substitute a source genre, allegation, stage of response, or accidental circumstance for an otherwise adequate subject category.

One story is sufficient evidence of *subject coverage* but is not evidence of frequency or of the accuracy of every claim in the historical report. Empty labels are evaluated separately.

### Extra singleton discovered during held-record adjudication

**HN-577 — 03.03 Policy debates and public response: KEEP.** The contemporary source reports street-level reactions to proposed thermonuclear weapons policy. The existing Executive actions leaf would imply that the executive act, rather than the public debate, is the focus. The Physics / Nuclear science branch would misstate a public-opinion article as a research achievement.

## Empty categories and browsing

Of the **361** third-level labels in the current lock candidate, **245** contain one or more primary articles and **116** have no primary article in this selected set. Those 116 are **unoccupied, not demonstrated redundant**. Two genuinely status-like second-level branches (original 06.07 and 06.08) were already retired; the original overlapping storm-only vessel-loss leaf was already broadened. The evidence does not support deleting all remaining empty topics as if this collection were the universe of potential historical news.

**Recommended navigation rule:** keep valid empty labels in the extensible taxonomy schema, but do not display empty leaves in a public browse menu until an item is assigned. This keeps a long-term classification structure without presenting 116 empty destinations to readers.

## Final reconciliation

The current candidate remains **13 main categories → 100 subcategories → 361 third-level labels**. The preserved CSV has **1,056 distinct, continuous HN identifiers**, valid candidate paths for each record, 21 generic editorial stories rerouted by subject and four previously held articles assigned with explicit qualifications. Original newspaper-source records and prior audit drafts were not modified in this review.

**Assessment: ready for a version-one taxonomy lock decision.** No one-story proposal warrants further merger or deferral based on the reviewed evidence. This does not certify source historical claims, guarantee taxonomy completeness for future newspaper additions or publish the category tree.
